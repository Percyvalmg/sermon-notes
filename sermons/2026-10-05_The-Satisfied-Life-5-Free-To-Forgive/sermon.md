## INTRODUCTION

We're continuing on **the satisfied life.** Say it with me: *the satisfied life.*

We've been taking our text from Philippians 4. Paul says he has learned, in whatever state he is, to be content.

> **Philippians 4:11-13**
> "Not that I speak in regard to need, for I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content. I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."

The New Living Translation says, *"I have learned the secret of living in every situation, whether with a full stomach or empty, whether with plenty or little."* **You can do it, neighbor. You can do it.**

Today we take it to the next level. Our text is Colossians 3:13. And our subtitle for this part of the series is **free to forgive.** Say it with me: *free to forgive.*

> **Colossians 3:13**
> "Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if anyone has a complaint against another; even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do."

**Must do.** Not try to do. *Must do.*

> **Ephesians 4:31-32**
> "Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, and evil speaking be put away from you, with all malice. And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you."

The satisfied life says, *I have learned to be content.* We have learned that **Christ is enough.** Our satisfaction is not in what we possess. It's not in circumstances. It's not in people's approval. It's not in success. It's not in getting everything we want. Our satisfaction is found in Christ.

And when Christ is enough, something begins to change on the inside of you. We stop living to get. We start living to give.

When we discover Christ is enough, we realize that because we have Christ, **we are never empty-handed.** We always have something to give. I may not have everything, but I have something. I have time. I have attention. I have compassion. I have a testimony. I have faith. I have a prayer. I have the Holy Spirit. And I have Jesus.

Whatever our status — money or no money, healthy or unhealthy — we can say, *"What I do have, I give to you."* Even Jesus, while He was on the cross, being tortured, dying, still gave of Himself.

So we stop living to get, and we start living to give. And that also means we start living to **forgive.** Part of the word *forgive* is the word *give.* Forgiving is also generosity.

So here's the question: can Christ still be enough if someone betrays us? Can Christ still be enough if someone disappoints us? Can Christ still be enough if someone lies about us? Can Christ still be enough when the apology never comes — when the person who hurt us carries on with their life as though nothing happened?

Because it is possible to have Jesus in your heart while still carrying somebody else in your head. You love God, but every time their name is mentioned, something stirs on the inside. You worship, but you're angry. You pray, but you're replaying the conversation while you pray. You smile, but you're carrying offense.

But Jesus wants you to be free. **A satisfied life is a free life.** Forgiveness is not just about setting the other person free. Forgiveness sets *you* free.

This is a big subject, because everything about Jesus is about forgiveness. Christianity is about forgiveness. Love is forgiveness. So it's something we have to specialize in. It's something we have to understand.

---

## POINT 1 — HURT IS REAL

We must never pretend that people haven't hurt us. **Jesus never teaches us to deny the pain.**

Some wounds are deep. Betrayal is real. Rejection is real. Abandonment is real. Abuse is real. Words can wound. People you trusted disappoint you. People you helped, people you sowed your life into, gave money to — they don't even know how much you did for them, and they turn on you in a moment.

No one is perfect. There's always a time when somebody hits rock bottom and says something stupid. Let's be real — none of us are Jesus. And that's exactly why we allow one another through the doors. **We're family.** We love one another. We forgive one another. We help one another.

But forgiveness does not say it didn't hurt. **Forgiveness says, "It hurt me, but I refuse to allow what happened to control the rest of my life."**

Joseph understood this. His brothers betrayed him. His brothers wanted to kill him — the other eleven wanted him dead, except the oldest brother saved him. They threw him into a pit. They sold him into slavery. He lost years of his life because of what his brothers did to him. But eventually, one day, Joseph could stand before those same brothers:

> **Genesis 50:20**
> "But as for you, you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good, in order to bring it about as it is this day, to save many people alive."

Notice Joseph did not call their evil *good.* He said, *"You meant evil."* **Forgiveness does not rewrite history. Forgiveness doesn't call wrong right.** But forgiveness refuses to allow yesterday's wrong to control tomorrow's destiny.

God will take the evil. God will take the offense. And He'll turn it around.

---

## POINT 2 — UNFORGIVENESS KEEPS US CONNECTED TO THE PERSON WHO HURT US

This is the tragedy of unforgiveness. Someone hurts you, and then you carry them around everywhere for the rest of your life.

They aren't in the room, but they're in your mind. They aren't speaking to you, but you're having arguments with them. You may have forgotten what happened, but you're reliving it every day. They hurt you once, but your memory hurts you over and over and over. **Unforgiveness allows someone to live rent-free in your heart.**

You keep replaying it. *What should I have said? Could I have said it differently? How could they do that to me? I hope one day they get what they deserve.* And now you're just waiting for God to take them out.

And slowly, bitterness begins to take root.

> **Hebrews 12:15**
> "Looking carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled."

A root is underground. You can't see it. Nobody sees it. But eventually, what's underground starts producing something above ground. This root becomes anger. This root becomes suspicion. This root becomes cynicism. This root becomes distrust. This root becomes harshness, revenge, and bitterness.

That's why we need to deal with the root. Shout it out: *Deal with the root.* Talk to yourself. Say, *"Me — deal with the root."*

**Don't let something somebody did to you become something that lives inside of you.**

---

## POINT 3 — UNFORGIVENESS IS A DEBT MENTALITY

Jesus speaks about forgiveness using the language of debt. We read this in Matthew 18. Jesus tells the story of a servant who owed an enormous debt he could never repay. He went to the king, and the king forgave him all of it. But then that same servant went out and found someone who owed him a much, much smaller debt:

> **Matthew 18:28**
> "But that servant went out and found one of his fellow servants who owed him a hundred denarii; and he laid hands on him and took him by the throat, saying, 'Pay me what you owe!'"

After he had been forgiven everything. *Pay me what you owe.* That's unforgiveness. *Pay me what you owe.* That's unforgiveness.

Some of us are controlling people. You gave your cousin a hundred rand, and your cousin still hasn't paid it back. *"Oh pastor, it's not about the money, it's about the principle."* What principle? The principle is **forgive. Write it off.** You've been chasing that hundred rand, and while your focus was on it, God could have given it to you three hundred times over. You concentrate on that million you need to get back, and God could have given you another ten million if you'd just written it off. Do you know how much you've lost because of the attention you've given to what you were owed?

So forgive your cousin. Forgive your uncle. **Write off the debt.** Bless them and say, *"I sow it into your life."*

But it's not only money. *You owe me an apology. You owe me an explanation. You owe me the years I lost. You owe me my reputation back. You owe me for embarrassing me. You owe me for betraying me. You owe me for what you did to my family. And until you pay, I will not release you.*

There's the problem. **Some people can never pay you back.** No apology can return the years. No explanation can erase what happened. No amount of revenge can heal a wound. So if your freedom depends on them paying their debt, **they control your freedom.**

Jesus says, *release the debt.* Not because there wasn't a debt — there was. But because you've made a decision: *I will not spend the rest of my life collecting a debt that Jesus has called me to release.*

Turn to your neighbor and say, *"Release the debt."* Then point at yourself and say, *"Self — release the debt."*

---

## POINT 4 — WE FORGIVE BECAUSE WE HAVE BEEN FORGIVEN

Why do we forgive? **Because we have been forgiven.** That's the heart of a Christian. That's our text:

> **Colossians 3:13**
> "...even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do."

Our forgiveness begins in remembering His forgiveness in our lives. Before we talk about what somebody did to us, we remember what Christ did for us.

The gospel says: *I had a debt I could never repay, but Jesus paid a debt He did not owe. I was guilty, but He forgave me. I was lost, but He found me. I was sinful, but He showed me mercy. I deserved judgment, but He gave me grace.* Undeserved.

And when you understand how much you have been forgiven, you begin to see the offenses of others differently. Jesus wasn't minimizing what others did — He was magnifying what He did for us.

**Forgiven people forgive people.** Grace received becomes grace extended. Mercy received becomes mercy extended. Forgiveness received becomes forgiveness extended. We're the extended hands.

---

## POINT 5 — THE CROSS IS OUR MODEL

Look at Jesus on the cross. He's been betrayed, falsely accused, mocked, beaten, humiliated, and crucified. But from the cross — from the worst place Jesus could find Himself — Jesus says:

> **Luke 23:34**
> "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do."

This is Christianity. The world says, *"Hurt those who hurt you."* The mafia says, *"Do unto others before they do unto you."* But Jesus says, **love your enemies.** The world says get even. Jesus says forgive. The world says make them pay. But Jesus paid the price.

**The cross breaks the cycle of revenge.** Someone hurts me, so I hurt them. They hurt me again, I hurt them back again. And then generation after generation starts carrying the same anger. Families stop speaking. Brothers become enemies. Churches divide. Communities divide. Nations divide. Everyone remembers what the other side did, and now everyone has a reason to hate.

But someone has to stop that cycle. The question is: **will it be you? Are you the cycle breaker?**

At the cross, Jesus stopped the cycle.

This is where we live. There is a very fine line between justice and revenge — a point where it's no longer justice, it's revenge. People stir up hate to pull others into their camp, so they can get power and money. They don't care about anybody. As the church, we will be a voice on these issues in the name of Jesus. We won't play cheap politics. We won't play with the lives of our people. You are wise. You are Christian. Don't get pulled into this.

Someone has to be the Christian who says, *"It was done, but we forgive it. It stops here. Let's sit down. Let's look for a future. Let's not go back."* Because if you keep pointing fingers, pointing fingers, pointing fingers, you're always going to stir up hate. You take a wound that has healed into a scar, and you scratch it and scratch it until it bleeds again.

**Somewhere, someone has to forgive.** How do you break the cycle? Forgiveness. How do you break the cycle? Forgiveness.

That's enough points for today. There are another seven to go — looks like this is going to become a series.

## CONCLUSION & DECLARATION

We're not denying or belittling what you've gone through. Never. We're not forgetting the hurts of the past. We're not denying where we come from and what we came out of. But we know what we came out of — **and we can't go back there.** We can't let anyone divide us. Not on our watch.

We forgive. We forgive. And we have to continually forgive — every day. When? Every day.

This is not a feeling. **It's a decision.** Your emotions follow your choice. No matter how I feel, I'm going to forgive. I'm going to release. And when you forgive, God heals your heart. He heals those wounds. It's real pain — it's deep — but God is a God who heals, and He wants to set you free from the pain you've gone through.

*(A word of clarity: forgiveness does not mean returning to a place of abuse or subjecting yourself to abuse. That's not what we're talking about. But there has to be forgiveness.)*

**Prayer of release:**

> *Heavenly Father, I forgive those that have sinned against me. I release them, in the name of Jesus. Thank You, Lord, that as I forgive them, You also forgive me. You take this burden off my shoulders. You take this pain I've been carrying for years, the hurt within my heart, these deep wounds — You heal them today. It's my prayer, in Jesus' name.*

**Declaration:**

> *Heavenly Father, I trust Your word. Because Christ has forgiven us, we choose to forgive. We choose not to live in bitterness, anger, or offense. We choose not to be prisoners of our past. We release those who have hurt us. We release the debt. We surrender revenge unto God. We refuse to allow yesterday's pain to determine tomorrow's purpose. Our hearts are free. Our minds are free. Our families will be free. Our future is free. We choose mercy. We choose grace. We choose love. We choose forgiveness. Christ is enough for us. We do not need revenge to make us whole. We do not need an apology to complete us. We do not need man's approval to satisfy us — because Christ is enough. We have been forgiven. We are healed. We're moving forward by the grace of God. We are free to forgive, in Jesus' name. Amen.*

## SALVATION CALL

Maybe there's somebody here today who has never given their life to Jesus. It's impossible to live this life of love and forgiveness without God. Only God can give you that capacity.

That's where it starts. Calling yourself a Christian doesn't make you a Christian. Coming to church doesn't make you a Christian. **You must be born again.**

> **John 3:3**
> "Unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God."

To be a child of God, you have to be born of God. You have to turn from your life and lifestyle and say, *"Lord, I'm done. Here I am. I submit to You. Come dwell in my heart. Change me. Forgive me. Set me free."* And God will come. He'll change you — your nature, not just your behavior. You can modify your behavior to a degree, but you can't change your nature. You can't change who you are. But God can change you.

The Bible says now is the time of salvation. Not tomorrow — now. When God speaks to you, respond now. There are people who woke up this morning who will not see the end of this day, and they don't even know it. If you die today, is your life right with God? None of us are guaranteed tomorrow. You might leave this place and never sense your need for God like this again. But one day you will stand before God — five years, ten years, forty years from now. Please do not reject the voice of God.

**Prayer of salvation:**

> *Dear Jesus, I need You in my life. Please forgive my sin. Take out this old nature. Fill me with Your Spirit. Everything that I am, I give to You. My whole life I surrender unto You. Thank You, Lord, that You change me, You transform my life, and I will never be the same again. I am forgiven, changed, and cleansed, in Jesus' name. Amen.*

> *And Lord, I pray over each and every one who has prayed this prayer: every power of the devil broken over their life, every curse removed. I thank You, Lord, that You lead and guide them in all things, that You set them free from their past, and You lead them into Your purpose and direction, in Jesus' name. Amen.*

**You now belong to Jesus.**
