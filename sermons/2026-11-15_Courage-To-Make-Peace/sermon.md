# COURAGE TO MAKE PEACE

**Speaker:** Bert Pretorius
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Source:** 3C Church (3C Live Experience)

---

## INTRODUCTION

This morning we're talking about **the courage to make peace** — dealing with conflict God's way.

Over the last few weeks we've been looking at recognising the voice of the accuser versus recognising the voice of God. But within that, we also have to know how to deal with issues, how to deal with offense, how to deal with the emotions and the practical side of navigating life and dealing with things within our lives.

Our text today is **Matthew 18:15–20**:

> **Matthew 18:15–20**
> "Moreover if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother. But if he will not hear, take with you one or two more, that by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established. And if he refuses to hear them, tell it to the church. And if he refuses even to hear the church, let him be to you like a heathen and a tax collector. Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven. Again I say to you that if two of you agree on earth concerning anything that they ask, it will be done for them by My Father in heaven. For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them."

There are spiritual consequences for the way you speak. There are spiritual consequences for how you deal with situations.

**Conflict reveals the level of our maturity.** Where there are relationships, there will be disagreements. We have different backgrounds, different expectations, different ways of communication and interpretation. Sometimes we misunderstand one another. But also, sometimes we genuinely sin against one another.

Being a Christian does not mean we will never experience conflict. **Following Jesus changes how we respond to it.** Spiritual maturity is revealed in how we treat people when something has gone wrong.

We can worship together on a Sunday and struggle to speak to one another on a Monday. We can pray for revival while refusing to deal with the resentment in our own hearts. But Jesus gives us a way.

In Matthew 18, Jesus speaks about humility. He speaks about caring for little ones. He speaks about seeking the wandering sheep. Then He speaks about how we address sin in one another's life, and then He speaks about **forgiving** one another. The heart of this whole chapter shows us that God cares about people, and He wants us to walk in restoration and reconciliation.

## POINT 1 — MAKE RESTORATION YOUR GOAL

In Matthew 18:15, Jesus says if your brother listens, **you have gained a brother**. So what is the goal? The goal is to gain a brother.

Before you enter into a difficult conversation, you have to assess your own motive. You have to deal with your own life first. Ask yourself:

- Do I understand what actually happened?
- Am I willing to acknowledge my own contribution to the conflict?
- Do I really want this resolved — or what am I trying to get out of it personally?
- Am I genuinely prepared to forgive?

It is possible to win an argument and damage a relationship. You can damage your relationship with your wife, your husband, your parents, your children — for life — just because you need to be right. **Being right is totally overrated.**

It is not about being right. It is about restoring the relationship — with your boss, with the person who works for you, with your cell leader. It's possible to prove your point and humiliate somebody that Jesus loves.

How you address the problem has to reflect how much **God** values the person, not how much you value being proven correct. This does not mean pretending wrongdoing doesn't matter — restoration requires truth. But we speak that truth with a desire to seek healing, restoration, and change.

> **Galatians 6:1**
> "Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted."

Restoring someone — gaining a brother, gaining a sister — requires **gentleness** first, and secondly, a watchfulness over your own heart. Before you go into the conversation: remember, I also need grace.

## POINT 2 — GO TO THE PERSON PRIVATELY

The Bible says go to the person privately. Not go to WhatsApp. Not call a friend.

> **Matthew 18:15**
> "Go and tell him his fault between you and him alone."

Alone. Privately. You don't go and talk to everybody else. You talk to the individual. Jesus instructs us to address the matter between two people first. That's the biblical way of doing it.

Don't assume the person knows why you're hurt. They might not even know what they did, or how they've hurt you. Don't expect them to interpret your silence, your distance, or a cryptic social media post. Some people are just not wired the way you're wired — they can't read your mind, and they didn't read the situation the same way you did.

A conversation you keep avoiding becomes resentment you keep feeding. The Bible says don't let the sun go down on your wrath. As soon as the issue is there, deal with it. Don't give the silent treatment.

Go to the person in a spirit of gentleness and love — and know that the outcome might not be gentle, simply because of our humanity. But many conflicts grow because we discuss them with everybody else instead of the person involved.

When you speak to other people who were never witnesses to the situation, who don't really know the person, they start driving their own motive into your life — and your offense starts growing in a different direction, because now it's not just driven by you, it's driven by the people around you. The devil has a way of connecting offended people together, even people with nothing in common, so that your personal offense becomes fuel for someone else's cause. Over time, your own facts start to change in your own head — because you haven't had the conversation.

Taking the courage to go and speak to somebody directly is not easy. That's why it takes the power of the Holy Spirit to overcome the fear. Nobody loves conflict. But if it's affecting your heart, you've got to deal with it.

When we talk to other people instead, we gather opinions, repeat our version, and allow people to become offended over something they never witnessed and never experienced. Jesus says begin with a **private conversation**. That protects dignity and creates room for repentance without unnecessary embarrassment. We're not there for revenge or to embarrass — we're there to restore the relationship.

There is a difference between seeking wise counsel and gathering supporters. Wise counsel helps you handle a matter faithfully. Gossip spreads the matter even further.

It helps to begin like this: *"When this happened, this is how I understood it. Can you help me understand what you meant?"* — not *"This is what it is, you lied, you're a liar."* You may discover a misunderstanding. You may also discover a genuine sin against you. Either way, you have opened the door to truth.

## POINT 3 — SPEAK WITH HUMILITY, LISTEN CAREFULLY

> **James 1:19**
> "Understand this, my dear brothers and sisters: You must all be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger."

**Quick. Slow. Slow.** That's the formula for your conversations.

Listening means trying to understand — not staying quiet while you work out your response. That's not listening. Listening is putting yourself in the other person's shoes, to understand where they were at, what they were going through at that time, and to have empathy for what they were experiencing.

Going to someone is only the beginning. The spirit in which you go is what matters. You can follow the right steps with the wrong heart.

Before confronting another person, let God examine you first. Ask: *Lord, am I reacting out of wounded pride? Have I assumed motives? Is there some hidden thing in me that I need to deal with?*

> **Psalm 139:23–24**
> "Search me, O God, and know my heart; try me, and know my anxious thoughts; and see if there is any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."

Then ask: what have I done that I need to apologize for?

This is where we have to be honest about victim mentality in this generation — the idea that because something happened to you, you've never done anything wrong, your heart is pure, you're beyond reproach. That doesn't minimize what you've genuinely been through. But you can take real pain and start milking it, using it as an excuse to justify everything else that goes wrong in your life. That doesn't make it right, and it doesn't mean you have no issues of your own, or that you can't have a distorted heart.

> **Matthew 7:1–5**
> "Judge not, that you be not judged. For with what judgment you judge, you will be judged; and with the measure you use, it will be measured back to you. And why do you look at the speck in your brother's eye, but do not consider the plank in your own eye? Or how can you say to your brother, 'Let me remove the speck from your eye,' when you yourself do not see the plank in your own eye? Hypocrite! First remove the plank from your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother's eye."

With the same judgment you judge someone, God says you will be judged — with no grace. If that's the criteria you've set, that's the criteria you'll be judged by. A plank is big — if there's a plank in your eye, how are you even seeing the speck? First remove the plank. That doesn't mean you must never deal with the speck — but start with the plank. That's grace: going in with humility, knowing you don't have it all together.

When you address it, **be specific**. Avoid statements like *"you always"* or *"you never."* Marriage doesn't survive on *always* and *never.* And avoid *"everybody says"* — when it's really two or three people who found each other through the same offense. If someone tells me "everybody" has an issue, I ask them to name everybody — get the signatures, don't send me something anonymous. Not to embarrass or shame anyone publicly, but I need to be able to evaluate who is actually saying what.

Be specific. Explain in actual words what you know. Ask about what you don't understand. Don't presume.

Then **listen**. Listening doesn't mean you agree with everything that's said — it means you're willing to understand before you respond. Sometimes the way someone handled something still isn't right, but you can understand why they did it. And sometimes the words that bring healing are simple: *"I'm sorry, I misunderstood. You're right. I was wrong."* Or: *"Please forgive me."* Humility makes room for reconciliation.

**99% of conflicts get resolved by just following this first step** — going to someone privately.

## POINT 4 — INVITE WISE HELP WHEN THE MATTER REMAINS UNRESOLVED

> **Matthew 18:16**
> "But if he will not hear, take with you one or two more, that by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established."

Some matters cannot be resolved in one conversation. Jesus provides a next step: one or two others to help establish the facts, bring accountability, and ensure a fair process. This must be people who love truth and reconciliation — mature, discreet, and willing to challenge either party. Not your closest friends who already agree with you. Someone the other party will also trust.

This is not about gathering your own camp and ganging up on somebody. It's about trustworthy people who can raise the same concern with humility, who are able to say, "the accusation hasn't been established," or "you both need to take responsibility — you've misunderstood one another," and then put real accountability in place: behavior needs to change, the way you speak needs to change.

## POINT 5 — TAKE ACCOUNTABILITY SERIOUSLY

If someone still refuses to hear, the matter goes to the church. Now the church takes care of those who have been hurt and calls the person responsible to repentance. **Love tells the truth and follows through.**

The church must deal with the unrepentant person — someone who doesn't want to change, who keeps on hurting others. This is rare, but it does happen. And if the person still refuses to hear the church, Scripture says to treat them as you would an outsider.

Understand this: forgiving someone does not mean abandoning necessary boundaries. Where there is abuse, threats, or suspected criminal conduct, seek appropriate protection — if necessary, involve the authorities. Jesus's instructions here must never be used to demand a private confrontation that places someone in danger or conceals wrongdoing or criminality. A healthy church makes room for grace, truth, and accountability. A church that covers up sin and allows people to keep hurting people is unacceptable.

## POINT 6 — SUBMIT THE PROCESS TO HEAVEN

> **Matthew 18:18–19**
> "Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven. Again I say to you that if two of you agree on earth concerning anything that they ask, it will be done for them by My Father in heaven."

Even as the church makes a decision, the authority of the church always remains under the authority of Christ. Before you go to the conversation, **pray.** Get to God. Pray when you don't know what to say. Pray for the person who has hurt you — the Bible says pray for your enemies. Pray for the courage to acknowledge your own wrongdoing.

Agreement in prayer must grow out of submission to Jesus. **Two people agreeing on a false accusation doesn't make it true.** The agreement Jesus speaks of is agreement in His name, according to His word. We cannot honor the name of Jesus while refusing the character of Jesus. It's His presence that gives us the courage to face difficult matters and handle them faithfully.

## POINT 7 — FORGIVENESS IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

After dealing with conflict, Peter asks Jesus how many times he must forgive a brother who sins against him — seven times?

> **Matthew 18:21–22**
> "Then Peter came to Him and said, 'Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? Up to seven times?' Jesus said to him, 'I do not say to you, up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven.'"

Jesus isn't giving a number. Peter wanted a limit — Jesus says there isn't one. Forgiveness isn't a transaction; **it's a lifestyle.** It's who we are, it's what we do. We may not have chosen what happened to us, but we are responsible for what we do with the hurt. The offense may be real, the pain may be deep, the consequences may continue — yet Jesus calls us to forgive.

And why do we forgive? Not because the person apologizes. **We forgive because we have been forgiven.**

> **Colossians 3:13**
> "Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if anyone has a complaint against another; even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do."

> **Matthew 5:7**
> "Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy."

Having received such great mercy, we are called to extend that mercy.

> **Matthew 6:12, 14–15**
> "And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors... For if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses."

If you set the standard by which you'll judge others, God says He'll judge you by that same standard — and that removes His grace from every area of your life.

## CONCLUSION & CALL TO ACTION

A lot of conversations are going to be taking place this week. You've got the courage. With Jesus, you can do it.

*Lord, where others have hurt me, sinned against me, sown destruction in my life — whether on purpose or by accident — I release them. I forgive them. Lord, where I have failed, where I have done better, please forgive me.*

*By the grace of God, I will be a peacemaker. I will speak truth with love. I will listen with humility. I will take responsibility for my own actions. I refuse gossip. I refuse bitterness. I seek restoration and honor godly accountability.*

*Jesus will be Lord over my words, over my responses, over my reactions, over my relationships.*

*We release personal revenge into Your hands. Help us to forgive, establish wise boundaries, and pursue restoration where it is possible.*

*Lord Jesus, rule over our homes. Rule over our church. Rule over our community. Make us a people who can handle conflict in a way that honors You. In Jesus's name. Amen.*

## SALVATION CALL

It's impossible to live this way — to forgive and die to yourself like this — without Christ. We don't have it in our own humanity. We'll all fail. But if you give your life to Jesus, He places that ability within you.

Calling yourself a Christian doesn't make you a Christian. You must have an encounter with God. You have to repent and say, *Lord, I'm done being my own god, following my own opinion.* Come before the Lord, and He'll take out that old nature, place His Spirit within you, and you'll never be the same again. But you have to make the choice to turn away from your old life and invite Him in.

*Dear Jesus, I need You in my life. Please forgive my sin. Take out this old nature. Fill me with Your Spirit. Everything that I am, I give to You — my whole life, I surrender to You. Thank You, Lord, that You forgive my sin, that You change me, that You make me brand new. And as I have received You and believe, I have the right to say I am a child of God. I am Yours, and nothing can snatch me out of Your hand, because I belong to You, in Jesus's name. Amen.*

None of us have a guarantee of tomorrow. Today is the day of salvation.
