# THE SATISFIED LIFE: WHAT YOU HAVE
**Speaker:** Charné Pretorius
**Series:** The Satisfied Life (Part 3)
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Source:** 3C Church (3C Live Experience)

---

## INTRODUCTION

We are wrapping up this series on the satisfied life. Done with frustration. Done with disappointment. Done with always wanting more. And entering into a place of **contentment**.

**Philippians 4:11-13 (NLT)**
> "Not that I was ever in need, for I have learned how to be content with whatever I have. I know how to live on almost nothing or with everything. I have learned the secret of living in every situation, whether it is with a full stomach or empty, with plenty or with little. I can do everything through Christ, who gives me strength."

*I have learned the secret.* There is a secret, and it is contained in the word of God. There is something hidden to human understanding that can only be revealed by the Spirit of the Lord. To those who trust Him, who believe in Him, who yield their entire lives to Him — they get to participate in the secret life of contentment.

Don't you just want to be satisfied? Don't you want to put your head on the pillow and say, *"Christ is enough. No matter what I'm facing, no matter my problems, no matter my situations — I am fully aware of the facts. But here is my truth: Christ is enough."*

Today we conclude with point nine. Turn to Acts chapter 3.

**Acts 3:4-6**
> "And fixing his eyes on him, with John, Peter said, 'Look at us.' So he gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them. Then Peter said, 'Silver and gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk.'"

When we step into the revelation that Christ is enough, we will always be in a position where we can **give** — even when we seemingly have nothing.

---

## POINT 1 — WHEN CHRIST IS ENOUGH, YOU ALWAYS HAVE SOMETHING TO GIVE

Have you ever looked at your life and thought, *I have nothing to give?* Maybe you looked at your wallet. Your level of education. Your level of influence. Your level of wealth. And you concluded you had nothing.

But look at the lame man at the gate. He was carried there every day by friends or family and placed where the traffic was heavy, so people would give him money to sustain his life. Notice this before anything else: **he did not come to the temple to find God.** He did not come for spiritual things, or the spiritual people, or for God Himself. He came — he was carried — so he could be given money.

The honest response most of us would give is, *"Sorry, I don't have anything."* In a country where people are begging at the mall, the parking lot, the gym — everywhere you turn, someone is in need. And often we don't want to engage, so we say, *"I don't have."*

And we aren't lying. In a day of cards and digital payments, few of us carry cash. We really don't have what they're asking for. **But here is the thing — we have not what they want, but we have what they need.**

You may not have the physical, earthly, human thing that person is asking for. **But you have the very treasure. You have the secret of life. You have Christ. You have what they need.**

Peter did not say, *"I don't have what you want."* He said, *"I will give you what I have — which is not what you're asking for, but it is what you need."*

---

## POINT 2 — STOP

Peter and John were not wandering. They were on their way to the temple. They were on mission, heading to an appointment. And the first thing they did was **stop**.

How many of us stop? Have you stopped this week — for a stranger, for a beggar, for someone in need? Or did you say, *"I'm so sorry, I'm a little busy. I'm on my way to do God's business"?*

A content person, a satisfied person, does not live a selfish life — because they realize that **what they have is what is needed**.

Sometimes the greatest thing you can give is not money. It is **time**. We live in such a hurried culture. Everybody is in a hurry. Everybody is late. Everybody wants to get there before the next person. *"Sorry, don't have time. Not now."* That is the culture we live in.

But Jesus had important places to be and important things to do, and **He was never too busy to stop.**

- Jesus stopped for blind Bartimaeus
- Jesus stopped for Zacchaeus
- Jesus stopped for the woman with the issue of blood
- Jesus even stopped for children

When you give someone your time, you are saying: *You are important to me. You are valuable. You are significant. You are precious.* You may not be able to fix everything or solve every problem, but you can give someone your time — and when you do, you are giving the very gift Jesus gave you. **You are dignifying another life.**

---

## POINT 3 — SEE

Peter and John stopped where everybody else walked past. Then the Bible says Peter **fixed his eyes on him**.

How often have we looked at people but not seen them? We only see what we need in that moment. We interact with someone — a cashier with a name badge, the person right in front of us — without them being a person to us. We are not malicious. But life drives us to interact with people without treating them with dignity and respect, caring only about what's happening with us.

How many conversations have you had while scrolling? How many times have you said *"Mm"* at the table but didn't hear a word? **You don't give your attention. You don't see.**

Peter and John gave the man their full attention. And then they demanded his — because a beggar usually will not look up. Have you seen the spirit of poverty on a person? How degrading it is? Their hand is out, but their head is down.

Peter said, **"Look at us."** *We see you. We care about you. We recognize you are more than a beggar. You may be a disabled beggar to some, but you are a son of God to us. You are not invisible. You are not insignificant.*

**Matthew 9:36**
> "But when He saw the multitudes, He was moved with compassion for them, because they were weary and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd."

When Jesus saw, He looked beyond the surface. Though He had His own pressures, He looked past them and saw in such a way that it moved Him. When last did you look and were moved?

Because we have Jesus. Because we are content. Because the Lord is our shepherd and we shall not want — **will we stop? And will we see?**

---

## POINT 4 — GIVE WHAT YOU HAVE

When they stopped and saw, they did something about it. Too often we stop, we see, and we say, *"Can't help you. It's beyond me. It's too big, too complicated, too much for me."*

Did they have what he was asking for? No. But they realized they had what he needed. **You're not always going to have what people ask for, but you have what they need. So start by giving what you do have.**

What do you have? Look at how God works with "nothing":

- Moses only had a stick
- The widow only had a handful of flour
- David only had five pebbles
- A little boy only had five loaves and two fish

But God took one little stone and defeated a giant. He took a few drops of oil and multiplied it into many vessels. He took a staff and split an ocean and brought water from a rock. And in the hands of Jesus, two fish and five loaves became a meal for a multitude.

**God has given you something.** What is in your hand? What has the Lord gifted you with, entrusted to your care? It may look like nothing to the person next to you, but to God that little, insignificant thing can become an extraordinary instrument of miracles, signs, and wonders — **if you will release it, if you will trust Him, if you will give what you have.**

But someone has to stop. Someone has to see. Someone has to give what they have. **And then God will do the rest.**

---

## POINT 5 — USE WHAT YOU HAVE TO GIVE WHAT THEY NEED: JESUS

What did Peter and John give the lame man? They had faith. They had healing power in them — because of the Holy Spirit, because of Jesus, because of the word and the authority Christ had given them. They prayed, and he could walk.

But he received much more than healing. **He received a future. He received a hope. He received the living Christ.**

We start with what we have as a point of connection, so we can give what is actually needed. And what is actually needed is **Jesus** — the Savior, the healer, the deliverer, the protector, the provider.

Yes, we celebrate that the lame man walked. But much more than that — **he walked into heaven, where previously he would have gone to hell.**

Peter walked with Jesus. Peter heard the words of Jesus. Peter experienced the grace of Jesus. Peter saw the empty tomb and the resurrected Jesus. He was filled with the Spirit of Jesus. **And so when he met this lame beggar, he could give him this Jesus.** The same is true for us. If His word lives in us, if His Spirit lives in us, if His love has changed us, if His grace has restored us — we can be instruments of it in the lives of other people.

**Colossians 1:27**
> "...Christ in you, the hope of glory."

We look at the violence and crime on our doorstep and ask, *What can we do?* We can stop. We can see. We can take what we do have, and ultimately we can give Jesus. **He is the hope for our nation, our communities, our daughters, our sisters.** Christ in us, the hope of glory.

---

## POINT 6 — DO YOU WANT THE HEALING, OR THE HEALER?

There is nothing wrong with believing for healing. There is nothing wrong with asking for deliverance from infirmity — in fact, it is what you should do. Never accept sickness. Never accept disease. Never settle there, because **Jesus went to the cross so that you may be healed**.

But the Spirit of the Lord asks a piercing question: *"Do you want the healing, or do you want the Healer?"*

When the thing you are seeking becomes your obsession — when all your prayers, all your fasting, all your seeking is for the gift more than for the Giver — Christ is being pushed off the throne of your life. Not because you are evil. Because you are human. And the Lord loves you too much to let you put the healing above the Healer.

Remember the three Hebrew young men before the fire.

**Daniel 3:17-18**
> "Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and He will deliver us from your hand, O king. But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, nor will we worship the gold image which you have set up."

*My God can deliver me — but even if He doesn't.* And remember Job, who lost everything: his children, his wealth, his health, his good name, even the support of his wife. He had only the breath in his lungs.

**Job 13:15**
> "Though He slay me, yet will I trust Him."

*If He takes the last thing I have, yet will I praise Him.* This is the heart God is after. You must worship when you are sick. You must praise when you are not feeling well. You must rejoice in tribulation.

*"If I don't heal you, will it change you? Will you stop praying for the sick? Will you stop preaching the good news? Will you become suspicious of My goodness? Will you accuse Me of injustice?"*

The only right response is to humble yourself: *"Lord, forgive my arrogance. You owe me nothing. And You are enough. You are enough. You are enough."*

Nothing should take the place on the throne of your life. No matter how desperately you need something — **Christ must be enough.** This is freedom: not fearing death, not fearing Satan, not fearing hell, because you know Jesus is enough. Like Paul said, *"For me to die is gain."*

---

## CONCLUSION & CALL TO ACTION

For your marriage — Christ is enough. For your family, your work, your body, your mind, your ministry, for anything and everything that concerns you — **let Jesus be enough.**

Christ cannot be made enough through going to church. Going to church is not enough. Being a good person is not enough. Having money is not enough. None of those things can complete you. **The peace that surpasses understanding only comes when Christ is in you.**

As we prepare to partake of communion together, we declare:

> *"Lord, I am content. I am satisfied. I am at peace. Christ is enough for me. I will stop, I will see, I will give what I have — and I will give Jesus. If He slays me, yet will I praise Him. I know He can; but even if He doesn't, I will be a worshipper, a praiser, a devoted disciple-maker. In Jesus' name. Amen."*

---

## SALVATION CALL

A very moral, generous, religious man — one of the highest theological scholars of his day, a Pharisee — came to Jesus and asked how this works. Jesus told him plainly:

**John 3:3**
> "Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God."

You were born of your parents and became their child. You must be born of God to become His child. What is born of flesh is flesh, and what is born of Spirit is spirit. For Christ to dwell in you, for Christ to be enough, for you to be content and satisfied and unafraid of death — you need Jesus.

Going to church doesn't make you a Christian. Studying theology doesn't make you a Christian. **You must receive Jesus.**

**Romans 10:9**
> "That if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved."

Today is the day of salvation. Now is the appointed time. When you hear the gospel, you have an appointment with it — you either receive Him or reject Him. And maybe you have backslidden. The fire has died. You are going through the motions with questions and doubts. He wants to restore you. He wants to bring you back home.

**Salvation Prayer:**

> *"Jesus, I come as I am, and I repent of my sin. Forgive me — for where I have lived for myself, where I have not honored You, where I have not served You. I turn away from my old ways and I embrace You. I confess You as my Lord and my Savior, and I thank You that from this day I can say I am a child of God. Jesus lives in me. I am born again. Amen."*
