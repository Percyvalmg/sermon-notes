## INTRODUCTION

We're continuing with this series, speaking about **the satisfied life.** Have you tasted and seen for yourself that the Lord is good?

> **Psalm 34:8**
> "Taste and see that the Lord is good."

There's only one life that can truly satisfy, and that is the life in Christ. Say it with me: **Christ is enough. Christ is enough.**

> **Philippians 4:11-13**
> "Not that I speak in regard to need, for I have learned in whatever state I am to be content. I know how to be abased, how to have nothing. I know how to abound; and everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."

The NLT says, *"I have learned the secret."* Say it with me: *I've learned the secret.* This is a very precious secret that we need to get into our life, and it brings great contentment.

Last week we looked at the effect of a life of unforgiveness, and we laid down some points when it comes to forgiveness.

> **Colossians 3:13**
> "Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if anyone has a complaint against another; even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do."

Look at your neighbor and say, *bear with me. Bear with me.* **Christ is our standard.**

> **Ephesians 4:31-32**
> "Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, and evil speaking be put away from you, with all malice. And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you."

Get rid of these things. Get rid of bitterness. Get rid of wrath. Get rid of that anger inside of you. Get rid of that explosive clamor. Get rid of all evil speaking and all malice.

Now all these things He mentions — bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, evil speaking, malice — **these are all secondary issues.** They are not the primary thing. Many times you struggle with anger in your life and you pray, *Lord, deliver me from this anger. Deliver me from this outburst of wrath. I don't want to be like this.* And you confess, and you confess, and you confess — but you never come to the root problem.

You can treat the fruit of the tree, but unless you dig down and get to the root of the issue, you will struggle with those broken, messed-up fruits all the time. **Christ forgave us. And because we have received His forgiveness, we can also forgive other people.**

---

## WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW ABOUT FORGIVENESS

Satisfaction is found only in Jesus Christ. Because we are content in Him, because we have everything, we always have a lot to give to other people. When you step out of a parasite lifestyle — where it's all about getting as much as you can from everybody around you — when you step out of that because you found contentment in Christ, now you can become generous. Generous as an individual. Generous as a family. Generous as a community.

**And the greatest gift you can give someone is something they don't deserve: forgiveness.**

The Word says, *"I can do all things through Christ."* I can obey the Word. I can live a life that honors God. But here's the question: Is Christ still enough for you even when someone betrayed you? Even when someone disappointed you, or told lies about you in the workplace? Is Christ still enough even if they refuse to apologize, even if they don't feel sorry? Is Christ still enough even when people see you hurting and they don't care?

It's possible to have Jesus in your heart and still be consumed with someone else's words, actions, and conduct. **A satisfied life is a life that is truly free.**

Forgiveness is not about setting the other person free. It's about setting *yourself* free, where you are no longer captured, no longer bound by the lies and by what people did to you. Last week we laid down these points:

- **Hurt is real.** You can never really forgive someone until you acknowledge the devastation they caused in your heart. Until you acknowledge that you are broken, God cannot restore you. Forgiveness doesn't say *what you did didn't hurt me.* It says, *I refuse to allow what happened in my past to determine the rest of my life.* Joseph didn't brush off his brothers' betrayal — he acknowledged the pain, yet said, *"What you intended for evil, God turned around for good."*
- **Unforgiveness keeps us connected to the person who hurt us.** You carry the offense to your pillow. You toss and turn while that person sleeps like a baby. Every morning you wake up with that anger, the only person who gets hurt is you. **Unforgiveness is like drinking poison hoping someone else will die.** It lets someone live rent-free in your heart.
- **Unforgiveness is a debt mentality.** *For me to be okay, I need an apology. You need to take responsibility.* But some debts you simply need to release, because people can never pay back what they took. Say it with me: *Release the debt.*
- **We forgive because we have been forgiven.** We remember where we came from — a debt we could not pay, guilty, in a mess — but Jesus took our place. On the cross, betrayed and crucified, He said, *"Father, forgive them, they don't know what they do."* **The cross is our model.**

> **Hebrews 12:15**
> "Looking carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled."

Bitterness starts with a tiny, almost invisible seed. On the surface it looks small, but underneath is a whole root system. Before you can build a successful marriage, a life of fruitfulness with your family, you have to deal with the root first. Otherwise, in every unguarded moment, words will manifest, attitudes will manifest, actions will manifest — and they will cause destruction.

> **Proverbs 4:23**
> "Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life."

You guard your cell phone with a front cover, a back cover, and insurance — and that's a stupid cell phone. But when it comes to your heart, the Word says *guard your heart,* because out of it flow all the issues of life. Your marriage is the result of your heart. Your children are the result of your heart.

## POINT 6 — FORGIVENESS DOESN'T MEAN WHAT HAPPENED WAS ACCEPTABLE

Many people hold on to an offense because they think the moment they forgive, they align themselves with the agenda of the other person — as if what the other person did wasn't serious.

**Forgiveness doesn't mean there should never be consequences.** All actions have a reaction. Forgiveness doesn't mean justice doesn't matter. It doesn't mean you stop pursuing justice. And forgiveness doesn't mean you must immediately trust again.

Sometimes people sin against you, you forgive them, and then they expect business as usual. *Didn't you say you forgave me? What do you mean you don't trust me?* We need to understand what pain and hurt bring into people's lives. For some people there's a mourning they go through — they've lost trust in someone they valued.

> **Matthew 5:4**
> "Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted."

**Forgiveness is something you do before God first.** This is so important: if you try to reconcile with someone before you've been with God, you're trying to do something spiritual in the flesh — and the flesh can only provoke the flesh.

Forgiveness is where you go before the Lord and you don't deny what happened, where you don't brush it off as *ag, this is just a tiny seed, let me leave it.* That root system in my neighborhood started with one seed. On the surface it looks small, but when you try to pull it out, you need a bulldozer. And by that one seed, many of my neighbors have been defiled.

When you come before the Lord with your brokenness, there are two things He will never reject:

> **Psalm 51:17**
> "The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit, a broken and a contrite heart — these, O God, You will not despise."

You cannot put your brokenness down unless you've owned it. **You need to own your brokenness** — whether it's unfair dismissal, betrayal in marriage, the abandonment of a child, gossip, or someone trying to ruin your reputation at work.

> **1 John 1:9**
> "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."

Notice the second part — not only forgive, but *cleanse.* Sometimes we think we need revenge. No. What you actually need is **compassion.** When you understand that broken people break people — that they could do that to you because it was done to them — and you pour it out before the Lord, He turns your brokenness into a deep compassion for those who sinned against you.

> **Romans 12:18**
> "If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men."

Reconciliation requires two willing people. That's why reconciliation is not always possible — but most of the time it is. Whether people change or not, whether they apologize or not, whether they acknowledge or not, **you can still be free.** You don't have to live a captured life.

This miracle happens between you and God first, where God forgives you, forgives that person, and restores your heart. Now, with a restored heart, you can go and speak to the person. Whether they reject you, laugh at you, or repent — it won't affect you, because God has already done the work.

**Your freedom does not depend on their repentance. Your obedience does not depend on their apology.** I can forgive even when I never hear the words *I'm sorry.*

---

## POINT 7 — FORGIVENESS IS A DECISION BEFORE IT BECOMES A FEELING

Many people say, *I don't feel like forgiving them.* Of course you won't feel like it. Feelings are natural — and natural does not produce freedom. **Feelings cannot bring restoration and forgiveness. It's a decision.**

Jesus didn't say, *forgive them when you feel ready.* **Forgiveness is obedience.** It's like faith — faith is not a feeling, faith is a choice. You choose to believe and you choose to obey what God says. And when you make that decision, that's when God brings healing.

You may forgive someone today, and tomorrow those same emotions rise up again. You don't have to go and forgive them again. You just remind yourself: *I have forgiven. I have forgiven.* And if it keeps coming up, it means there's not healing yet — so it's time to get into your prayer closet again.

Release the debt. **Submit your emotions to your obedience. Don't wait for your feelings to give you permission to obey God.**

> **Matthew 18:35**
> "So My heavenly Father also will do to you if each of you, from his heart, does not forgive his brother his trespasses."

Jesus said, *if you don't forgive from your heart.* Forgiveness from the head is not enough — you must forgive from the heart. It's only when we bring our hearts before God and acknowledge where we are at that He can restore us, removing the anger, the fear, and all the nonsense fruit of that offense.

Do not open a door for bitterness. Say it with me: *don't open a door.*

> **Luke 17:1**
> "It is impossible that no offenses should come."

Offense will come — **don't take it.** Don't feed your offense. Don't rehearse it. You find yourself driving from here to Durban reasoning with yourself about it the whole way. Don't build your identity around it. It was not your sin — it was their sin. **Don't make what happened to you the center of your story.** Don't let it become the final chapter of your life. There's something much greater that God has installed for you.

> **2 Corinthians 1:4**
> "Who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God."

One day God will use that rubbish to fertilize someone else's garden. As you receive the comfort of the Lord, comfort other people with that same comfort.

---

## POINT 8 — STOP REHEARSING THE OFFENSE

Stop telling everybody about it. **Go and tell God about it. Let God deal with it.**

We always want to spiritualize gossip. *Don't you know what they did? This is what they said. Can you believe it? We need to pray for them.* It doesn't matter what jacket you put on it — **it's still gossip.** When you keep rehearsing it, the offense becomes your testimony instead of Jesus becoming your testimony.

Be careful who you speak to. What benefit is it to speak to someone about your hurt if all they do is pour more fuel on the fire? What wisdom is that?

> **1 Corinthians 13:4-5**
> "Love suffers long and is kind... does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil."

Love keeps no record of evil. Are you keeping record? How big is the list of your spouse, your child? *Ja, but yesterday. Ja, but in 1991, you remember…* It paralyzes people. It destroys their hope when you continually remind them of their failures.

> **Proverbs 17:9**
> "He who covers a transgression seeks love, but he who repeats a matter separates the best of friends."

There's a difference between processing pain and practicing offense. One moves toward healing; the other keeps reopening the wound. So make a decision: **I'm not going to build my future around what happened in my past. I'm not going to be marked by my past.**

---

## POINT 9 — THE SATISFIED PERSON DOESN'T NEED REVENGE

When you are content, when you know that God's got your life in His hand, you don't need revenge. You don't need their approval. You don't need their affirmation. You don't need their apology. You don't need their reconnection. You don't need them to be punished.

I've listened to people speak after the murderer of their child was executed, and they said they thought they would feel better — but they're not feeling better. We say, *I'll only be satisfied when they admit I was right.* But even when they admit it, if you still carry that seed of offense, somebody else's apology doesn't take away your need to repent of taking the offense.

Our satisfaction depends on Christ. **Christ is enough.**

- They may never understand you, but **Christ is enough.**
- They may never apologize, but **Christ is enough.**
- They may never acknowledge what they did, but **Christ is enough.**
- They may never restore what was lost, but **Christ is enough.**

The satisfied person can say, *I don't need revenge to complete me. I don't need your apology to complete me. I don't need everybody to understand my side of the story. Jesus Christ is enough for me.*

When Christ is enough, we can release people from the responsibility of making us whole again. **Wholeness is a result of Christ.** When you're broken, no human effort can restore — only Christ.

---

## POINT 10 — RELEASE THE REVENGE TO GOD

> **Romans 12:19**
> "Beloved, do not avenge yourselves, but rather give place to wrath; for it is written, 'Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,' says the Lord."

Vengeance is God's. It's not that you wish God would break their teeth and destroy them. That's not your desire. **Vengeance is the Lord's — which means God is your vindicator.** He will bring fairness into your life. He will bring justice.

**Forgiveness means I move the case out of my courtroom and place it into God's hands.** God saw what happened. God knows everything that was said behind your back, in front of you, in your face, in secret. God is your vindicator. You don't have to be the judge. You don't have to be the jury. You don't have to be the executioner. You just have to be dependent on God. **God's got your back.**

Forgiveness doesn't say there's no justice. Forgiveness says, **I am not God. Let God be God.**

## CONCLUSION & CALL TO ACTION

Wherever you find yourself, whatever challenges you face, **this message is not about the person who hurt you. It's about what you're going to do with what you've got** — the hurt, the disappointment, the abandonment, the betrayal. What are you going to do with that?

God wants to break the cycle of unforgiveness in our nation. God wants to break the infiltration of offense and hatred in your family and in your community. **We are the catalysts of reconciliation.** Say it with me: *I'm a catalyst.*

You may not be able to change what happened, but something does depend on you.

> **Romans 12:18**
> "As much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men."

You can choose to forgive. You can choose obedience. You can choose freedom. You can choose to release the debt. You can choose to stop rehearsing the offense. You can choose to stop demanding revenge. And you can place the matter into the hand of God — the One who loves you, the One who's on your side, the One who will vindicate you.

> **Psalm 107:20**
> "He sent His word and healed them, and delivered them from their destructions."

His word is *forgive.* As we accept His word and implement it, there will come physical healing in your body, restoration in your soul, in your family, and in your marriage.
