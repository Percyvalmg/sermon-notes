## INTRODUCTION

Standing strong. One of the greatest misunderstandings in modern Christianity is the belief that faithfulness to Christ guarantees ease and comfort. You're not here by accident. You're here because God has purpose for your life. You have an assignment.

The Bible has never promised us ease. But it's promised us something far greater — that if we stand for Christ, **you will be opposed**. He doesn't promise us ease, but he does promise us opposition.

Opposition is not necessarily a sign of failure. It's proof that we are following in the footsteps of our Savior.

> **John 15:20**
> "Remember the word that I said to you, a servant is not greater than his master. If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you."

If the world hated the sinless Son of God, why should we expect a standing ovation when we live for him?

**If we are truly standing for Christ, there will be resistance.** Not because we are offensive, but because truth confronts darkness. Not because we are hateful, but because light exposes what prefers to remain hidden.

Persecution is not a sign that something is wrong with your faith. Often it is evidence that your faith is visible. If you don't have persecution, it means you're not doing anything.

---

## POINT 1 — PERSECUTION IS NORMAL FOR BELIEVERS

You mustn't get a fright when persecution comes your way. When you go to war, you expect someone to shoot at you. You might get surprised at when it happens, but you know it's not a matter of if. **It's just a matter of when.**

> **2 Timothy 3:12**
> "Yes, all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution."

Notice the word **all**. It doesn't say pastors only. It doesn't say missionaries that go to other countries. Anyone who desires to live godly in Christ Jesus will experience pressure. Notice it does not say *might* or *could*. It says **will**.

In the early church, persecution was physical. Violent. In many countries today, people die for their faith. In our modern world it looks different, but it's still real.

**Forms of persecution today:**

- **Social marginalization** — pressure to redefine biblical truth. When you stand on God's word regarding marriage, identity, morality, and salvation, you get labeled intolerant, a bigot, old-fashioned.
- **Relational rejection** — friends drift away when you post about Jesus. Family rolls their eyes. Neighbors stop inviting you over because you're "not fun anymore."
- **Professional pressure** — overlooked for promotions. Pressured to compromise values to fit corporate culture. Losing clients who discover you won't compromise. Being labeled intolerant in performance reviews.
- **Cultural and legal persecution** — mocked on social media. Dragged through HR complaints for quoting scripture. Christian teachers and counselors silenced by new policies. Biblical truth labeled as discrimination.
- **Subtle persecution** — eye rolls, sarcastic jokes, being called "the pastor" or "the Jesus person" or "the happy clappy" at work. Character attacks. Misrepresentation. Public shaming. Cancel culture.

> **Matthew 5:11**
> "Blessed are you when they revile and persecute you and say all kinds of evil against you falsely for my name's sake."

> **Acts 5:29**
> "We ought to obey God rather than man."

Words can form persecution. Character attacks. Social media attacks. Misrepresentation. People take a little bit of truth, turn it around, and make it sound other than what it really is.

**Blessed are you.** When you are persecuted, you're blessed. Count yourself blessed when they revile you, when they attack your character, when they misrepresent you, when they lie about you.

---

## POINT 2 — PERSECUTION STRENGTHENS YOU

We've got to shift our perspective. Persecution is not opposition — **it is formation.**

> **James 1:2-4**
> "My brethren, count it all joy when you fall into various trials, knowing that the testing of your faith produces patience. But let patience have its perfect work, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking nothing."

That's what opposition does. That's what persecution does. It brings you to a place of perfection where you learn to be patient — which means you learn to absorb. **Maturity is the ability to absorb someone else's immaturity.**

All the energy comes and when it hits you, it dissipates. It doesn't get worse. It dies with you. That's your superpower.

**What persecution produces:**

- **It deepens spiritual muscle** — not shallow strength, but deep, real strength that does not go away
- **It clarifies what you truly believe** — pressure separates shallow belief from rooted faith. When faith costs you something, it becomes real
- **It strengthens your endurance** — the ability to stand and keep standing

> **Romans 5:3-4**
> "And not only that, but we glory in tribulations, knowing that tribulation produces perseverance, perseverance character, and character hope."

Tribulation produces perseverance — the ability to stand. Perseverance produces character. Character produces hope — an excitement for the future.

**When faith costs you something, it becomes real.**

You've got to know what you believe. You've got to know what you stand for. That's why it's important to study the word. Don't be a lazy Christian. Ask why. Everything — ask why.

The early church did not shrink under persecution. It expanded.

> **Acts 8:4**
> "Therefore those who were scattered went everywhere preaching the word."

There was persecution. They were all scattered. And wherever they were scattered to, they started churches. The whole world got saved. **What the enemy intended to silence them became the catalyst for multiplication.**

---

## POINT 3 — WE DO NOT HAVE TO FEAR PEOPLE

**Fear is the enemy's primary weapon.**

> **Matthew 10:28**
> "Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul."

Don't fear people. Never fear people. Human rejection is temporary. God's approval is eternal.

> **Proverbs 29:25**
> "The fear of man brings a snare."

When you fear people, it's a trap. You get captured by people, captured by institutions, captured by systems. Fear traps you into silence. Fear pressures compromise. Fear magnifies people's opinions beyond their authority.

> **2 Timothy 1:7 (Amplified)**
> "God did not give us a spirit of timidity, of cowardice, cringing, and fawning fear, but He has given us a spirit of power and love, and a calm and well-balanced mind, discipline, self-control."

**You're not out of control.** You're well-balanced. You have not received a spirit of timidity. Timidity means intimidation. **You will not be intimidated.**

Don't allow people to intimidate you. When you understand who holds eternity, you no longer tremble before temporary voices.

> **Acts 5:41**
> "They departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for His name."

The apostles, after being beaten for preaching, did not retreat. They rejoiced. They wore it like a badge. Peter, when he was crucified, asked them to hang him upside down because he was not worthy to die the way Jesus died. To talk like that, there must be some conviction. There must be faith.

---

## POINT 4 — BE A MODEL TO THE NEXT GENERATION

This is where legacy enters the conversation.

If we retreat when pressure rises, what example are we to others? If we compromise to avoid discomfort, what foundation are we building for those who follow?

**Train your children for persecution from a young age.** They must understand they are not like everybody else. When you are a leader, you lead. You're alone. A leader doesn't do what everybody does. Every Christian is a leader. If you're like everybody else, the term for that is average.

From a young age, teach them:
- You will be persecuted — you can't feel bitter or sorry
- You have a calling and an assignment
- When people mock you, wear it as a badge
- That means you're somebody. That means you're doing something

Sunday is just an outflow of your discipline from Monday to Saturday. The time you give to God, the time you give to your assignment — that's who you are.

They're watching you — whether you will stand with conviction or bend with convenience.

**A distinction:** This is not about persecution because you do stupid stuff. Self-inflicted persecution — because you were proud, arrogant, undisciplined in speech — that's on you. This is about persecution because you stand for Christ.

---

## POINT 5 — OUR RESPONSE TO A HOSTILE WORLD

We don't respond to hostility with bitterness or fear or compromise. We respond in a Christlike way:

- **Courage without arrogance**
- **Conviction without cruelty** — just because we believe a certain way doesn't mean we must be cruel to people struggling with themselves, their identity, their sin
- **Faith without fear**
- **Love without compromise**

**The greatest legacy we can leave is not comfort — it's courage. Not safety — it's steadfastness.**

Following Jesus is worth any price.

Christianity is not cultural convenience. You're going to be at loggerheads with your family, your culture, your upbringing — but you handle it right. Not with arrogance and pride thinking you're better than someone else.

**Our response:**
- **Remain faithful** — the world may reject us, but we remain faithful in Christ
- **Remain loving** — Jesus never stopped loving those who rejected him. If they reject you, love them more
- **Remain bold** — there's a difference between bold and arrogant. Opposition should not silence the gospel
- **Remain joyful** — joy in persecution

---

## POINT 6 — THE REWARD OF FAITHFULNESS

Persecution is temporary. Reward is eternal.

> **Matthew 5:12**
> "Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven."

> **Matthew 25:21**
> "Well done, good and faithful servant. You were faithful over a few things, I will make you ruler over many things. Enter into the joy of your Lord."

**That affirmation outweighs every criticism.**

Our goal is not the applause of people. It is the approval of the King.

Let every insult, rejection, and sacrifice fade into those words: *"Well done, good and faithful servant."*

If you're not being persecuted, it means you're not doing anything. Persecution will come your way. But through every persecution, God gives a way of escape. God has got your back. God will lead and guide you.

## SALVATION CALL

There's no way you can live an uncompromised life without Christ. There's no way you have this level of character apart from Christ within your life.

Coming to church doesn't make you Christian. Having Christian parents doesn't make you Christian. Calling yourself Christian doesn't make you Christian. The Bible says you must be born again. To be a child of God, you've got to be born of God.

The Bible says, "Repent and believe and you will be saved." Repent means you're done being your own God, living according to your opinion, self-righteousness — and turning to the Lord saying, *"Lord, I need you. I need your character. I need your forgiveness. I need your grace. I need your mercy. I want that conviction. I want that love."*

When you come to God, He'll forgive your sin. He'll cleanse you, change you, take out that old nature, place His Spirit within you. It's a miracle and you'll never be the same again.

**Salvation Prayer:**

> *Dear Jesus, I need you in my life. Please forgive my sin. Take out this old nature. Fill me with your Spirit. Everything that I am, I give to you. My whole life, I surrender unto you. Thank you, Lord, that you change me. You set me free. You make me brand new. I trust you. I trust your word. It says if I receive you and believe, I have the right to be called a child of God. And thank you, Lord — as from now, I belong to you and nothing can snatch me out of your hands, because I am yours. In Jesus' name. Amen.*
