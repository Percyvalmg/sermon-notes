# THE SATISFIED LIFE

**Speaker:** Bert Pretorius
**Series:** The Blessed Life (Part 2)
**Date:** 2026-08-31

## INTRODUCTION

Last week we looked at the blessed life. We saw that the blessed life is not a perfect life — it's a life that knows what to do when we have failed. This week we go to the next word: **the satisfied life.**

Christ is enough. Learning the secret of contentment.

> **Philippians 4:11**
> "Not that I speak in regard to need, for I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content."

Note those words: **I have learned.** It's not that the Holy Spirit fell on you and suddenly you knew. It's not a quick fix. There's a process.

> **Philippians 4:12-13**
> "I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."

The New Living Translation puts it this way: *"I have learned the secret of living in every situation, whether with a full stomach or empty, whether with plenty or with little."*

Verse 13 is probably one of the most quoted verses in Christianity. People put it on t-shirts. Athletes put it on their shoes. We quote it before we write exams. We use it when we believe God for a breakthrough. And there is nothing wrong with believing that Christ gives you strength. But when Paul wrote these words, he was not talking about achieving more. **He was talking about needing less.** He wasn't saying Christ will give me everything I want. He was saying Christ gives me strength even when I don't have what I want.

That changes the meaning. That's why Paul says, *I have learned the secret.* It's a secret because people don't know it. What did Paul discover? **Christ is enough.**

---

## POINT 1 — CONTENTMENT HAS TO BE LEARNED

Contentment doesn't automatically come with salvation. *"Now I'm saved, so now I'm content."* No. Paul learned it through experience.

We want a miracle. We want a quick fix. We want God to do something big and instant. But look how a child grows. When a baby is born, we don't pray, *"I speak development, maturity, growth — I declare it's going to be speedy. Instead of 18 years, it's going to be two years."* None of us pray like that. Why? Because we know it's a process. Eighteen years of feeding, of development, of education, of training, of modeling. It's not a quick fix.

> **Philippians 4:11-12**
> "I have learned... everywhere and in all things I have learned."

So we process. Whatever state I am in, I have learned to be content. And for some of us that process is a year or two. For some of us it's thirty, forty years.

**Say it: I have learned.**

---

## POINT 2 — PAUL KNEW BOTH SIDES

> **Philippians 4:12**
> "I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound... both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need."

Note this: Paul is not glorifying poverty. But he's also not glorifying prosperity. He says, *I know both.*

There are two tests in life: **the test of lack and the test of abundance.** We often think the greatest spiritual test is having nothing. But sometimes having everything is the greater test.

> **Deuteronomy 8:11**
> "Beware that you do not forget the Lord your God."

God warns Israel that when they have eaten and become full, built beautiful houses, multiplied their possessions and increased their wealth, their heart should not become proud. Why? Because sometimes we seek God differently when we need Him.

- When there is no answer except God — we pray.
- When there is no provision except God — we trust.
- When there is no door that opens except God — we depend on Him.

Some of us don't pray because we don't feel we need to pray. When we were struggling, we were in the cell group, doing the vision, focused. Then God blessed the business, and suddenly time became an issue. Suddenly we can't make church every Sunday. Suddenly we're too busy to pray. We take the blessing and it becomes our security. It becomes our idol. Now we lean on the very thing God gave us in the first place.

I would rather pray that God closes your business — so that you stay saved, so that your children grow up in a righteous home — than have you gain everything and lose what actually matters.

Because what is success? Success is raising your kids right. Success is staying married. Success is having a family that contributes, that puts in the work, that serves others. **I don't want to know what you have. I want to know what you've given away.**

---

## POINT 3 — SOMETIMES SUCCESS IS A GREATER TEST THAN SUFFERING

> **Deuteronomy 8:17**
> "Then you say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have gained me this wealth.'"

That's the danger. You start looking down on other people. Your influence and your money become your measure. Now you think you're more than someone else.

*"How did you achieve that?"* — *"Well, I worked hard."* Everyone works hard. That's normal. You're supposed to work hard. *"Well, I sacrificed."*

Consider the man who takes three taxis to get to work, because he chooses to stay near his family and be home at night. He works a hard day of labor, takes three taxis home to his shack. And the world calls him a nobody, unsuccessful — while the one standing next to a jet is called a success. What planet does that come from? Get off social media. That's not success.

Show me the people you are raising. Show me the investment you're making into the next generation. Not how much you have.

One of prosperity's greatest dangers is that **we begin taking credit for what grace produced.** We prayed for the business. God blessed it. Now suddenly it's all us.

---

## POINT 4 — GOD WILL SOMETIMES REMOVE WHAT WE DEPEND ON

There are seasons when God allows the things we trusted to be shaken. Not necessarily because He is against us, but because He is revealing what we have been depending on.

> **Hebrews 12:27**
> "Now this, 'Yet once more,' indicates the removal of those things that are being shaken, as of things that are made, that the things which cannot be shaken may remain."

So when your life gets shaken, what remains? *"Well, my money is gone."* But your wife is still there. Your family is there. They're serving the Lord. What are you valuing? Your faith remains. Your ability to touch people remains. Your ability to bring transformation into the lives of people remains. When everything is shaken, that which is of value remains.

Sometimes God has to shake our world so we can see what we've been depending on. Sometimes He lets a door close. Sometimes a relationship changes. Sometimes an opportunity disappears. Sometimes the resources aren't there. Sometimes the applause stops — nobody is patting you on the back, and suddenly all the so-called friends are gone.

That's when we discover where our confidence really was. Was it in my money, my position, my reputation, my connections, my ability, my ministry, my success — or was it in Christ?

**If losing the blessing makes us lose our worship, the blessing has become our god.**

> **Hebrews 13:8**
> "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever."

Our circumstances change. Our finances change. Our health changes. People change. Positions change. Opportunities change. But Jesus remains the same. And spiritual maturity is when our peace becomes increasingly dependent on who God is, rather than on what we are experiencing around us.

---

## POINT 5 — YOU ALREADY HAVE SOMETHING TO GIVE

God does provide. Even in the wilderness, God provides. But there's no other source of provision but God. And it comes through a process, until you learn.

So the question is not *how do I get more?* The question is *what can I do with what I have?*

> **Acts 3:6**
> "Silver and gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk."

*What I do have.* Don't tell me you don't have anything — that's a lie out of the pit of hell. To say you have nothing is to be unthankful, full of ingratitude, and entitled. You are wealthy on the inside. Even when you're struggling with yourself, struggling with your emotions, struggling with mental health, struggling with your body — that struggle is where you learn dependence on God. And even there, you still have something. You can still sow. You can still give.

**Say it: I have something. Christ is enough.**

## A WORD ON MERCY

*(Before the message, Pastor Bert addressed the congregation on how believers treat one another.)*

We want the mercy of God. We want to be forgiven. But so often we carry no mercy toward others.

> **Matthew 5:7**
> "Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy."

> **Matthew 7:1-2**
> "Judge not, lest you be judged. For with what judgment you judge, you will be judged; and with the measure you use, it will be measured back to you."

Everyone wants to be an influencer, but they've built nothing and helped nothing — so they dig into people's failures and expose people at their worst. Where is the mercy? Here is the confusion: you say you're a Christian, you want the mercy of God for yourself, but you can't forgive the person next to you, and you show their weaknesses to the whole world.

> **John 13:35**
> "By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another."

Love starts with forgiveness. That's how we know Christ loved us — He died on the cross and forgave our sins.

This doesn't mean we cover sin or turn a blind eye to unrighteousness. We deal with it. But the Bible gives an order:

> **Matthew 18:15**
> "If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother."

Go to the person first. Give them a chance to repent and to fix it. Don't run to the boss and destroy someone's livelihood. If they won't hear you, then bring one or two others. There are ways and ways. Expose corruption, yes — but show mercy in how you do it. Remember, David was a murderer, and he's the one who wrote *"The Lord is my shepherd."* So don't get caught up in being high and mighty. We are not God's fruit inspectors.

The church will always have the messy and the broken, because we welcome the worst of the worst — that's the God we serve, a God who forgives. **This is a safe place for imperfect people.** But we don't tolerate sin, because sin destroys you. So we don't cover up. We deal with things, and we protect the vulnerable.

## CONCLUSION & DECLARATION

I had to learn this — not in a week, but over years. It's not easy. But with God, all things are possible.

And when someone asks how you're doing while you're in the middle of the struggle, you don't have to pretend. You can simply say: *"I'm learning."* I'm learning not to be disappointed. I'm learning not to be depressed. I'm learning to be patient. I'm not there yet — be patient. And we're all in it together, including me. We're all learning.

So it's not about needing more. It's about what you can do with what you have. Be patient. Be dependent. Learn the secret.

**Declaration:**

> *Heavenly Father, we declare today that Christ is enough for us. We will not be controlled by possessions, positions, recognition, or success. We will remain faithful in abundance and faithful in need. We will enjoy God's blessings without worshiping the blessing. We will carry success without pride. We will walk through difficulty without losing faith. Our identity is in Christ. Our security is in Christ. Our strength is in Christ. Our future is in Christ. Whatever comes, whatever goes, whatever — Jesus remains. And through Christ who strengthens us, we can face every difficulty, because You remain faithful. In Jesus' name. Amen.*

**Say it: Christ is enough.**

## SALVATION CALL

There may be somebody here who has not yet given their life to Jesus. That's where it starts. It's not about religion — it's about a relationship with a God who loves you. He is not a prosecutor standing over you, or a judge ready to destroy you. He is a loving Father who loves you and cares for you.

But there comes a time when you have to make the decision to receive Him as your Father, receive Him as your God.

> **John 3:3**
> "Unless a man is born again, he cannot enter the kingdom of God."

To be a child of God, you have to be born of God. Calling yourself a Christian doesn't make you a Christian. Coming to church doesn't make you a Christian. You have to repent from the old lifestyle of rebellion, living according to your own opinions and thoughts, and say: *"Lord, I'm done with that. My life according to me is over. I'm going to trust You."*

God will come in. He'll change your life. It's a miracle. You'll never be the same again. But you've got to make that choice to invite Him into your life.

Jesus says now is the time of salvation. Another translation says *today.* When God speaks to you, take it seriously, because you might not get another chance. There are people who woke up this morning who will not see the end of this day. You don't know when your time is. Don't reject the voice of God.

**Prayer of salvation:**

> *Dear Jesus, I need You in my life. Please forgive my sin. Take out this old nature. Fill me with Your Spirit. Everything that I am, I give to You. My whole life I surrender unto You. Thank You, Lord, that as from today I will never be the same again, because You forgive me, You cleansed me, You changed me, You set me free from my past. And as from now, I belong to You. I am Your child. In Jesus' name. Amen.*
